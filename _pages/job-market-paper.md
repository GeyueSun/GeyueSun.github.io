## Not Just Talk: Crypto Activity, Equity Repricing, and Macro-Financial Transmission

### Abstract

Aggregate spillovers from crypto returns into U.S. equities appear muted, but how crypto shocks reach traditional financial markets depends on which firms are exposed and how they are linked. Using large language models, I classify SEC Forms 10-K, 8-K, and 20-F covering **6,551 U.S. domestic and foreign private issuers**, distinguishing crypto mentions from firms' actual crypto and stablecoin activities. In **7.05 million firm-day observations** across 3,879 U.S. domestic issuers, Bitcoin-related equity repricing is concentrated in firms with substantive crypto activities: return sensitivity nearly triples from general disclosures to core activities, while mention-only firms show none. Repricing is strongest for proprietary holders, smaller firms, and financial firms, and Bitcoin returns also affect operating income and asset growth. Through industry links, Bitcoin movements also reprice firms without direct exposure, more strongly in business services and less in banking. Stablecoin activity, by contrast, is mostly reserve holding and service provision rather than payment settlement. These findings show that financial stability surveillance must look beyond aggregate market correlations to identify where crypto exposure is concentrated and how it spreads across industries.

### Key Contributions

- **New measurement.** Uses LLMs to classify 10-K, 8-K, and 20-F filings (2015–2025) for 6,551 issuers, separating firms that merely *talk* about crypto from those with their own crypto activity, and own activity from *core*, price-linked activity (treasury holdings, mining, trading, custody, stablecoin provision). Each statement is dated by its public filing time, so the daily indicators use only information investors could observe.
- **Markets reprice activity, not talk.** A 10 percent rise in Bitcoin is associated with an additional stock return of about 0.45 pp for firms with any crypto disclosure, 0.85 pp for firms with their own activity, and 1.28 pp for firms with core activity; mention-only firms show no sensitivity. Repricing is strongest for proprietary holders, smaller firms, and financial firms.
- **Real effects.** Bitcoin returns also feed into corporate operating income and asset growth.
- **Industry transmission.** Through industry links, Bitcoin movements reprice firms without direct exposure — more strongly in business services and less in banking — while stablecoin activity is mostly reserve holding and service provision rather than payment settlement.
