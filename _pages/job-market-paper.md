## Not Just Talk: Crypto Activity, Equity Repricing, and Macro-Financial Transmission

*Geyue (Grace) Sun &nbsp;·&nbsp; Job Market Paper (2026) &nbsp;·&nbsp; Advisor: [Prof. Tomas Williams](https://tomas-williams.com/)*

[**Email me for the latest draft**](mailto:geyuesun@gwu.edu) &nbsp;·&nbsp; [Related NBER working paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34475)

### Abstract

Aggregate Bitcoin spillovers into U.S. equities appear muted, but this paper reveals substantial heterogeneity beneath the market average. Using large language models, I classify SEC Forms 10-K, 8-K, and 20-F covering **6,551 U.S. domestic and foreign private issuers**, distinguishing crypto mentions from firms' actual crypto and stablecoin activities. In **7.05 million firm-day observations** across 3,879 U.S. domestic issuers, Bitcoin-related equity repricing is concentrated in firms with substantive crypto activities: incremental Bitcoin–equity return sensitivity nearly triples from general crypto disclosures (0.045) to core activities (0.128), while mention-only firms are not sensitive at all. Among crypto-active firms, Bitcoin sensitivity is strongest for proprietary holders, smaller firms, and those in the finance industry (0.171). Bitcoin returns also affect corporate operating income and asset growth. Yet indirect equity-market links follow a different pattern: as more industry peers engage in crypto, Bitcoin movements reprice non-exposed business-services firms more strongly and non-exposed banks less. Stablecoin activity, by contrast, is mostly reserve holding and service provision rather than payment settlement. These findings show that financial stability surveillance must look beyond aggregate market correlations to identify where crypto exposure is concentrated and how it spreads across industries.

### Key Contributions

- **New measurement.** Uses LLMs to classify 10-K, 8-K, and 20-F filings for 6,551 issuers, separating firms that merely *talk* about crypto from those with actual crypto and stablecoin activities.
- **Heterogeneous repricing.** Shows that Bitcoin–equity sensitivity nearly triples from general crypto disclosures to core crypto activities, and is strongest for proprietary holders, smaller firms, and finance-industry firms.
- **Real effects.** Documents that Bitcoin returns feed into corporate operating income and asset growth.
- **Indirect transmission.** Finds that industry-peer crypto engagement reprices non-exposed firms — more strongly for business services, less for banks — and that stablecoin activity is mostly reserve holding and service provision rather than payment settlement.
